# Contributing to the Positive Rate Method

This methodology is the author's own synthesis — assembled from known,
proven ideas (see doc 14), not invented from nothing and not simply
extracted from any one build — that crystallized while developing a real
project and is validated the same way it was built: **on real projects,
not in theory.** Contributions are welcome under that standard.

## The validation rule

Amendments to the docs are accepted on evidence, not elegance. The strongest
contribution is a **departure-map finding or friction-log entry from a real
adoption** (see doc 11 and `/templates`): what the doc said, what your
project needed instead, what it cost you to find out. "This would be neater
if..." without a project behind it will be discussed but rarely merged.

## How to contribute

- **Discussions** — open to everyone. Questions, adoption stories,
  hard-path problems, disagreements with the docs. This is also where you'll
  find others who hit your problem first.
- **Issues** — one issue per friction point or proposed amendment. The
  preferred format is a **feedback packet** finding: if you adopted via
  ADOPT.md, your agent already keeps `method-feedback.md` — say "package
  feedback" and paste what it produces. Each finding carries its doc, its
  class (DOC-BUG / GAP / FRICTION / POSITIVE), and proposed amendment
  text where one exists.
- **Pull requests** — welcome for doc amendments, template improvements, and
  case-study submissions. PRs that change a discipline's substance should
  reference the issue and the real-project evidence behind it.

## Contributor agreement

By submitting a contribution you agree that:

1. Your contribution is your own work or you have the right to submit it.
2. You license it under the repository's license (CC BY-SA 4.0).
3. You additionally grant the maintainer a perpetual, non-exclusive right
   to use, modify, and relicense your contribution as part of the Positive
   Rate Method and derived teaching materials.

Item 3 is the standard open-methodology arrangement: it keeps the project
free to evolve (including into courses and printed material) without
tracking down every past contributor for permission. Your attribution is
preserved in the git history and, for substantial contributions, in the
docs themselves.

## The triage ritual (how findings become law)

The method evolves the way its constitutions do — by explicit amendment,
never by drift. Incoming feedback packets are triaged on a standing
rhythm, agent-assisted, human-approved:

1. Every finding receives exactly one written disposition: **amendment
   committed** (with the finding referenced in the commit message),
   **fenced** (a procedure/red-flag added rather than a doc change), or
   **no-change, with the reason written in the issue.** Silence is the
   only prohibited outcome — the incident-log charter, applied to the
   method itself.
2. Three independent findings of the same class against the same doc
   force the doc onto the triage agenda regardless of individual merit.
3. POSITIVE findings are tallied — they are the method's own
   positive-rate evidence and feed the case studies.
4. Amendments ship as versioned commits; the git history is the method's
   legislative record.

## Case studies

The most valuable contribution of all. If you shipped something under the
method — especially if it replaced payroll hours — a case study in the
`/case-study` shape (callouts costed at start, what got built, headline
number, what failed) earns a permanent place in the repo. Honest failure
detail is required, not optional; that's what separates a case study from
an ad.

## Support expectations, stated plainly

This is a donation-supported open methodology, maintained by a working
business operator. That means:

- Discussions are read and curated on a **weekly rhythm**, not on demand.
- Repeated questions become published articles, not repeated answers.
- There is **no support obligation** attached to using the method, donating,
  or contributing.
- Hard-path problems that outgrow the docs (migrations, scaling walls,
  platform pivots) are exactly the ~1-in-20 case doc 08 names — the honest
  answer is to get **real expert technical help**, not to keep flying it
  yourself or expect an agent session to resolve it. This project doesn't
  broker or sell that help; naming the boundary honestly is the whole
  obligation.

## What will be declined

- Contributions that rename or rebrand the methodology
- Vendor promotion dressed as amendment
- Complexity without a costed callout behind it — the method's own
  ornamental-abstraction rule applies to the method itself
